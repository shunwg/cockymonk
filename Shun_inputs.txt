# Addendum — Art direction guardrails minimal, human, and lightly rat-themed

This is a direction constraint, not a locked art specification.

The Snifferoo theme must be clean, minimal, and contemporary. The rat, maze,
trail, and cheese ideas are a lightweight visual frame for a human social word
game—not a literal cartoon world that appears in every component.

The intended feeling is
- A clever, social word game for adults and mixed-age groups
- Lighthearted and occasionally funny
- Calm enough to read, think, bluff, and vote
- Warm enough to invite table talk and playful accusations
- Visually confident, with space for the words and player names to lead

The intended feeling is NOT
- “Cute mouse game”
- “Cheese game”
- A children’s app
- Animal role-play
- A rodent cartoon, mascot universe, or character comedy
- A busy themed interface where every button has ears, tails, cheese, or maze art
- A fixed visual recipe that prevents design exploration

## 1. Open-ended art direction

Do not lock the final palette, texture, rendering technique, mascot style, or
illustration treatment in this PRD.

The implementation team may explore
- Warm or cool dark base palettes
- Flat graphic, lightly tactile, illustrated, or restrained 3D-inspired surfaces
- Geometric, hand-drawn, or abstract maze motifs
- Minimal rat tokens, symbolic pawns, or very simplified rat silhouettes
- Quiet paper, stone, space, or terrain material cues
- A restrained amount of motion and sound polish

All exploration must preserve the functional visual contract below.

The design should aim for “minimal but memorable,” not “maximally themed.”
When uncertain, remove decoration before adding decoration.

## 2. Theme is a seasoning, not the interface

Use the rat-and-maze theme selectively.

### Strong thematic moments
The theme may be most visible in
- App icon and launch identity
- Board view
- Pawnavatar tokens
- Small transition moments between round phases
- Board movement, overtakes, landmarks, and winner screen
- Optional ambient details in Fjellet and Verdensrommet
- Small score-reward moments, where cheese represents earned points

### Quiet functional moments
The theme must be subdued in
- Bluff writing
- Game Master truthdecoy composer
- Voting
- Rules
- Settings
- Reconnection
- Profile
- Accessibility interfaces
- Any screen where long text, timing, privacy, or a consequential choice matters

A player should be able to use these screens comfortably even if all decorative
art, animation, and sound are removed.

## 3. Rat usage rules

Rats are player tokens, not a recurring cast.

Requirements
- Show rats primarily where player identity, board position, movement, or
  social comparison benefits from an avatar.
- Keep rats small, stylised, and readable rather than expressive cartoon
  characters.
- Prefer a simple pawnsilhouette treatment over faces, poses, dialogue, or
  personality animation.
- Use player names and colouraccessibility markers as the primary identity;
  the rat is secondary.
- In Verdensrommet, rats wear space helmets. In Fjellet, they may read as
  mountain mice through silhouette or environment, not through costumes.
- Do not make rats speak, narrate, react in speech bubbles, make jokes, or
  explain rules.
- Do not create a named rat protagonist, mascot, helper, villain, or host.

Avoid repeated rat imagery in
- Buttons
- Form fields
- Navigation
- Every empty state
- Every notification
- Every answer card
- Every piece of instructional copy

If a screen still works and feels like Snifferoo after removing a rat illustration,
that is usually the correct amount of rat imagery.

## 4. Cheese usage rules

Cheese is a score metaphor, not the dominant visual material.

Requirements
- Keep numeric points visible alongside any cheese representation.
- Use cheese mostly for score gain, milestone, board-goal, and end-of-game
  feedback.
- Prefer a small, abstract, graphic reward treatment over oversized cheese
  illustrations.
- Use cheese as an occasional payoff a crumb, token, marker, glow, or
  destination—not wallpaper.

Do not
- Fill backgrounds with cheese
- Add cheese to ordinary buttons or menus
- Make every point counter a pile of cheese
- Use cheese puns in every string
- Let cheese obscure score readability

## 5. Humour rules

Humour must come primarily from the players’ own believable bluffs, wrong
guesses, accusation, reveal, and social reactions.

The UI may support this with
- Dry, short supporting lines
- Good timing around reveals
- Small unexpected-but-subtle visual beats
- A satisfying overtake or score movement
- One restrained celebratory moment for a great bluff or Game Master win

The UI must not try to be funny all the time.

Avoid
- Constant puns about rats, cheese, smells, tails, holes, crumbs, or vermin
- “Lol random” animation
- Loud cartoon sound effects
- Slapstick rat reactions
- Insulting players for choosing incorrectly
- Overly childish phrases
- Long flavour text that slows the table down

Copy rule
 Core instruction first. Optional flavour second. If the flavour makes the
 action less clear, delete the flavour.

Examples of acceptable restraint

 Context  Norwegian primary  English primary  Optional flavour, only if space permits 
------------
 Bluff entry  `Skriv en løgn`  `Write a bluff`  `Gjør den troverdig.`  `Make it believable.` 
 Vote  `Hva er sannheten`  `Which one is true`  None required 
 Correct answer  `Du fant sannheten. +2`  `You found the truth. +2`  `God nese.`  `Good instinct.` 
 Bluff success  `3 stemte på løgnen din. +3`  `3 players chose your bluff. +3`  `Pent spilt.`  `Nicely played.` 
 GM win  `Spillmesteren vant runden.`  `The Game Master won the round.`  `Ingen fant sannheten.`  `Nobody found the truth.` 

Examples to avoid
- `Cheese! You smell like victory!`
- `Squeak! Another silly mouse fell for it!`
- `Rat-tastic bluff!`
- `Oopsie, you took the stinky trail!`
- Any copy that makes an adult player feel they are using a children’s app

## 6. Minimalism and hierarchy

The game must retain generous negative space and a strong reading hierarchy.

For every screen
1. Make the current phase unmistakable.
2. Make the one primary action unmistakable.
3. Make the word, answer, player name, score, or timer that matters now easy
   to find.
4. Add thematic decoration only after the first three are successful.

Hard limits
- One dominant visual idea per screen.
- One main CTA per screen.
- One primary semantic accent colour per state.
- Avoid multiple competing textures, illustrations, borders, shadows, and
  badges in the same viewport.
- Never add theme decoration behind answer text or input text.
- Avoid using card-within-card-within-card layouts.
- Keep maze lines thin, low contrast, and away from critical text.
- Keep backgrounds quiet enough that screenshots remain readable at a glance.

The word game is the visual hero. The theme should frame it, not compete with it.

## 7. Colour and material flexibility

Do not prescribe a final colour palette in the PRD beyond accessibility and semantic
requirements.

The visual designer may explore colour and material direction, provided that
- Text contrast meets accessibility requirements.
- Semantic feedback remains consistent during a game.
- Player identity colours remain distinguishable without relying solely on hue.
- Themes remain recognisably different without changing the board’s functional
  layout.
- The interface does not become visually noisy or overly saturated.
- The main reading surfaces remain calm and legible.
- Goldcheese-like reward treatment, if used, is restrained and not required
  on every screen.

The codebase should use semantic design tokens, not hard-coded aesthetic colour
names. Example token categories

- `background.primary`
- `surface.reading`
- `surface.secret`
- `text.primary`
- `text.secondary`
- `action.primary`
- `state.confirmed`
- `state.pending`
- `state.private`
- `state.warning`
- `player.one` through `player.eight`
- `board.path`
- `board.goal`

Exact values and visual treatments are intentionally open for the design pass.

## 8. Motion and sound restraint

Motion, haptics, and sound should make state changes feel physical and satisfying,
not constantly demand attention.

Use stronger feedback only at
- Vote submitted
- All bluffs received
- Voting opens
- Truth reveal
- A bluff receives votes
- Game Master round win
- Pawn advancement
- Overtake
- Board milestone
- Final win

Keep feedback quiet or absent at
- Typing
- Ordinary navigation
- Waiting
- Reading instructions
- Repeated list interactions

A single well-timed motion, sound, and haptic is preferable to layered,
continuous effects. Effects must remain meaningful and consistent across both
board themes.

## 9. Visual review test

Before accepting a new screen, ask

1. Could someone understand the current action without recognizing the rat theme
2. Does the screen still feel like Snifferoo if the rats are only tiny player
   tokens
3. Is the joke or charm coming from player interaction, rather than decorative
   animal content
4. Is the wordanswerplayer state more visually prominent than the maze,
   cheese, or rat art
5. Is there one clear primary action
6. Would an adult feel comfortable putting this on a table with friends
7. Can a player using large text, VoiceOver, Reduced Motion, mute, or high
   contrast still complete the action
8. Have we removed at least one decorative element that was not doing useful
   work

If any answer is “no,” simplify before adding new art or copy.

## 10. Definition of done

The Snifferoo visual direction succeeds when players describe it as
- “A clever word game with a great board”
- “Funny because of what we said to each other”
- “Clean and easy to follow”
- “The little rats are a nice touch”
- “It feels like a proper game night”

It has failed if players describe it as
- “The mouse game”
- “A cheese app”
- “A cute kids’ game”
- “A maze game”
- “Busy”
- “Hard to read”
- “Trying too hard to be funny”
