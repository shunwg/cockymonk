Every intentional Lab deviation from the frozen demo gets a dated row. A divergence "graduates" only when PRD.md or DESIGN.md is amended to match — until then it ships nowhere.

| 2026-07-27b | **The door stays open all game, and the waiting room stops lying.** `netJoinOpen()` drops the 3-minute clock — only a finished game refuses (`LATE_JOIN_MS` and `G.joinOpenUntil` retired). A newcomer takes a bot's chair immediately, or, if no bot is sitting, waits in `G.pendingSeats` and is dealt in by `newRound()` at the current last-place score. Waiting screens gain a spectator marker: `WAIT`'s green ✓ and `VOTEWAIT`'s "du stemte" now render only for someone who actually submitted. New pure policy in net.js (`netSeatKind`, `netStartScore`), and `netProject`/`netBroadcastState` now serve a seatless watcher instead of skipping them. | The window was the wrong shape for the product: a session runs 20 minutes and people wander in, so a door that shuts at minute three turns an ordinary social arrival into a refusal. The queue is not a compromise — a new chair genuinely cannot appear mid-round, because `gmForRound()`, `winCheck()` and `scoreRound()` all read `playerCount`, so the round boundary is the only safe moment; the waiting room the player experiences and the invariant the engine needs are the same rule. The banner fix is the game-feel rule (principle 2) applied to its plainest breach: an effect claiming a game state that is false — *your lie is in* — on the first screen a newcomer ever sees. Last-place-not-zero because a fresh chair on zero at round nine cannot catch the table, which is a spectator seat with extra steps. | Graduated (PRD §5.5, amended 2026-07-27b) |
| 2026-07-27 | **The last-five-seconds siren.** Inside `TIMERS.URGENT_MS` the screen edges pulse `bluffPink` on a 330 ms half-cycle sine, with one soft falling two-tone and one `warning` haptic on entry. Gated on **inaction**: it fires only on BLUFF, VOTE and GM_DASH — the screens where the player still owes an answer — and never on a waiting screen, which excludes timed-out players and latecomers for free. Torn down by `resetTimers()` with the phase. | Ported from Ordkrig, where it is the single most effective thing in the build: you feel the deadline instead of reading it off a number. Three of their decisions kept exactly — sine in and out (a linear ramp reads as a strobe), 330 ms half-cycle, and a trough that never reaches zero, which is what makes it a siren rather than a blinking light. Two changed: theirs is a flat 0.5 fill over a monochrome app, which on our paper-on-dark cards would push option text under 4.5:1 exactly when it must be read (DESIGN §9), so ours is an inset vignette that leaves the middle clear; and theirs runs unconditionally, while ours rests at the trough under Reduced Motion so the beat still lands with sound and haptics carrying it. ~1.5 flashes/second, under WCAG 2.3.1's three. Ordkrig's countdown is silent — the audio leg is ours. | Graduated (PRD §5.2a, DESIGN §7/§9) |
| 2026-07-27 | **A bot's lie is another card's truth.** `Lab/js/fakepool.js` replaces the flat draw from `fakes_*.json`: for word *W* the candidate lies are the `truth` fields of the other deck cards, and a round's lineup takes about half by a closeness score (length ratio to the truth, shared initial, matching latinate-vs-folk morphology, shared content words) and the rest at random. The card in play, every played card and the next 16 draws are barred so a lie can never resurface as a truth; on a deck too small for that the exclusion relaxes instead of emptying. `fakes_*.json` survives as an emergency net, reached in 0.0% of lineups. | The demo's fakes were written to a different spec from its truths and nobody chose the consequence: nb truths average 75 characters and the fakes 48, so **"vote for the longest option" won 87.2% of a 4-option practice lineup** (65.6% en) against 25% chance. Drawing the lie from the same corpus as the truth makes surface form match by construction rather than by tuning, so the leak cannot creep back as the deck grows. Method ported from Ordkrig (`src/bots/answerPool.ts`), not its CC BY 4.0 corpus. Measured after: 26.2% nb, 25.1% en. No scoring or flow change; engine vectors untouched. | Graduated (PRD §9.1) |
